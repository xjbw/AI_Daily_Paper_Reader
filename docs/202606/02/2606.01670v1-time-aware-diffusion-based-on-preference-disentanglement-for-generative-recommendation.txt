## Abstract 

Recently, Generative Recommenders (GRs) have emerged as a trans-formative recommendation paradigm by replacing traditional item IDs with semantic indices (SIDs). Owing to the exceptional genera-tive capabilities of diffusion models, a few pioneering works explore developing GRs with diffusion architectures as the backbone. How-ever, a fatal limitation of existing diffusion-based GRs is that the diffusion process applies uniformly to all items within the historical interactions. In contrast, the user preference is shaped by multi-faceted time-evolving factors and thus exhibits a non-stationary distribution in the temporal aspect. To bridge this gap, this study proposes a novel GR framework, named TDPM, by designing the time-aware diffusion on SID tokens. Specifically, TDPM explicitly integrates the impact of time-evolving user preferences into the diffusion process. In detail, the user preference is disentangled into 

(i) the period preference , which remains consistent over a long time-span, and (ii) the point preference , which is triggered by recent focal events. Extensive experiments on three public real-world datasets demonstrate the significant superiority of TDPM over the state-of-the-art baselines. TDPM achieves average improvements of up to 29.21% and 25.45% in terms of HR@20 and NDCG@20, respectively. The ablation study further underscores the necessity of time-aware token diffusion in diffusion-based GRs. Our code and datasets are available at https://github.com/Sylphy666/TDPM.

## 1 Introduction 

Driven by the great success of generative models across diverse domains [ 6, 14 , 30 , 34 ], recommender systems have increasingly shifted toward generative modeling [ 3, 15 , 22 , 38 ]. In contrast to conventional recommenders that rely on multi-stage cascade pipelines [ 8, 13 , 28 , 29 , 39 ], generative recommenders (GRs) cast recommendation task as next-token prediction problem. On the basis of discrete semantic indices (SIDs) [ 22 ], GRs can directly gen-erate the next item a user is most likely to interact with, providing a unified and scalable approach to user preference modeling. In current literature, GRs mostly leverage pretrained language models (PLMs) [ 22 ] or large language models (LLMs) [ 35 , 36 ] as the gener-ative backbone, to exploit the collaborative information inside the user historical behaviors. Recently, a few pioneering works [ 24 , 25 ]have adopted diffusion models as the backbone to achieve SID-based generative recommendation, primarily motivated by their remarkable efficacy and efficiency [10, 16, 27, 30]. While being promising, existing diffusion-based GRs [ 24 , 25 ]remain relatively rudimentary. Most of them integrate standard diffusion models [ 10 ] directly into the generative recommenda-tion pipeline. To be more specific, their forward diffusion process treats all items uniformly and indiscriminately and is completely item-agnostic, devoid of the essential task-specific customization required for recommendation scenarios [ 24 , 25 ]. However, due to the influence of the time-evolving user preferences [ 12 , 32 ], items in user interaction sequences differ significantly from the funda-mental units [ 2, 10 ] in typical diffusion processes, such as visual pixels [ 10 ] in images or textual tokens in sentences [ 2]. User pref-erences, underlying the chronological interaction sequences, are shaped by a multitude of factors, such as personal characteristics and external environments [ 1 ]. To bridge the research gap, this study proposes a novel diffusion-based generative recommender called TDPM 1, by developing the time-aware diffusion based on preference disentanglement. Specifi-cally, during the diffusion process, TDPM explicitly takes the time-evolving user preference into consideration. Hence, TDPM can not only adaptively adjust the intensity of diffusion process, but also elegantly capture the contribution of each historical item to the rec-ommendation results. In detail, when modeling the time-evolving characteristic of user preference, TDPM disentangles it into a period preference , which encapsulates the stable user profile, and a point preference , which captures the user transient short-term intents. Typically, period preference is gradually accumulated across a long time-span, thus exhibiting a steady evolutionary pattern along the temporal dimension. On the other side, point preference accounts for abrupt interest deviations within a narrow temporal window, which is induced by external effects, such as emergent hot topics and stimuli. Afterwards, the period preference and point prefer-ence synergistically determine the diffusion intensity for each item, rather than the naive uniform diffusion. Eventually, TDPM suc-cessfully adapts the standard diffusion process into the time-aware diffusion based on preference disentanglement for GRs. • In order to effectively capture the time-evolving property of user preference, this study proposes a novel diffusion-based generative recommender system, TDPM. ## 2.2 SID-based Generative Recommender 

The GR paradigm based on semantic indices (SID), leverages item se-mantic representations to construct discrete and structured SID for each item. And GR subsequently models the probability distribution over the user interaction history in SID space, rather than directly in the raw ID space [ 35 , 36 ]. By compressing high-dimensional semantic features into compact discrete SID, SID-based approaches substantially mitigate the scalability challenges inherent in con-ventional GRs while preserving semantic proximity among items. Concretely, this paradigm comprises two principle modules, SID Generation and Recommendation-oriented Finetuning [22, 35]. 

2.2.1 SID Generation. The objective of this module is to produce discrete tokens as SID for each item, grounded in its rich semantic features (e.g., textual descriptions and visual attributes). The gener-ation process typically unfolds in two stages. First, for an arbitrary item 𝑖 𝑗 , an off-the-shelf pretrained embedding model (e.g., LLMs or multi-modal LLMs) is employed to extract a dense semantic embedding vector 𝑒 𝑗 ∈ R𝑑 . Second, to compress the continuous embedding into discrete SID, a vector quantization algorithm, such as Residual Quantized VAE (RQ-VAE) [ 35 , 38 ] or Residual Quanti-zation K-Means (RQ-KMeans) [ 36 ] is employed over the entire set of item embedding vectors {𝑒 𝑗 | 𝑗 = 1, . . . , | 𝐼 |} . This procedure yields a multi-level, coarse-to-fine discrete SID for each item. The resulting SID is denoted as a token sequence (𝑠 (1) , . . . , 𝑠 (𝑚 ) ) with 𝑚 

denoting the length of SID and each 𝑠 (𝑘 ) , 𝑘 = 1, . . . , 𝑚 belonging to a finite vocabulary determined by the corresponding quantization layer [ 22 , 35 ]. Notably, this hierarchical structure endows the SID space with a semantic tree topology, where items sharing common prefixes exhibit stronger semantic similarity.

## 2.3 Diffusion Model 

Diffusion model is a class of deep generative models grounded in non-equilibrium thermodynamics [ 10 , 26 , 27 ], which has achieved remarkable success across various generation tasks [ 2, 9, 23 , 33 ]. The framework comprises two complementary processes: a for-ward diffusion process that gradually perturbs data by adding noise into pure noise, and a reverse process that reconstructs data by iteratively removing the added noise [10]. This design rep-resents the intuition that more recent interactions carry greater predictive weights for next-item recommendation [ 19 ], as they bet-ter capture the user’s current interest state. Accordingly, later items in the interaction history are assigned with larger period prefer-ence values, enabling the model to prioritize temporally proximate signals when modeling long-term preference consistency.

## 3.3 Optimization 

In practical implementation, we usually utilize a warm-up stage before the time-aware token masking stage, which draws inspira-tion from the pretrain-finetune paradigm of large language model training [ 4, 11 , 21 ]. The warm-up stage functions as a pretraining phase, allowing the model to develop a general understanding of the SID vocabulary and its co-occurrence patterns through stochastic masked token reconstruction. Building upon this, the time-aware token masking stage serves as a task-specific finetuning phase, where the model learns to perform next-item recommendation, precisely the objective of the inference phase. At the warm-up stage of training, we employ a uniform token masking strategy, wherein all the items in the interaction history share an identical masking probability 𝑝 ,

𝑝 1 = 𝑝 2 = · · · = 𝑝 𝑛 = 𝑝. (13) To improve the training stability, the identical masking probability 

𝑝 is not fixed but sampled from a bounded uniform distribution, 

𝑝 ∼ U ( 𝑝 min , 𝑝 max ), (14) where U denotes the uniform distribution, 𝑝 min and 𝑝 max denote the lower and upper bound of the uniform distribution respectively. This stochastic sampling of 𝑝 exposes the model to varying de-grees of data perturbation, enabling stability and robustness across different masking ratios and building a solid foundation for the subsequent time-aware token masking stage. Once the masking probability 𝑝 is determined, each token in the SID sequence is independently masked according to 𝑝 , and the resulting masked sequence is then fed into the diffusion model for the reverse prediction. The training objective of the warm-up stage is almost the same as the time-aware token masking stage, adopting similar loss function but without the weighting mechanism, 

L𝑊 = −Σ𝑚𝑛 𝑗 =1I[ ˜𝑆 𝑢 ( 𝑗 ) = [M] ] · 𝑙𝑜𝑔𝑝 𝜃 (𝑆 𝑢 ( 𝑗 ) | ˜𝑆 𝑢 ), (15) where all terms retain the same meanings as in Equation 12. Serving as a pretraining phase, the warm-up stage is essentially designed to establish a foundational understanding of the SID space before the more intricate task-specific time-aware token masking stage. Specifically, it equips the model with the basic capability of recovering masked tokens based on the partially observed SID sequence, analogous to the masked aotoencoding paradigm [ 28 ]. By exposing the model to a simple and uniform masking schedule, the warm-up stage guides the model parameters toward a well-conditioned initialization, in order to prevent the following time-aware token masking stage from collapse. In essence, the warm-up stage provides a stable pretrained model for the downstream finetuning phase with the task of next-item recommendation. Once the warm-up stage establishes a stable initialization, the training advances to the time-aware token masking stage, where the uniform token masking strategy is replaced by the proposed time-aware token masking strategy. In the time-aware token masking stage, the perturbation undergoes a sharp escalation in difficulty. Specifically, the masking probability shifts from being sampled from a simple bounded uniform distribution to being dynamically determined by the fused preference of period preference and point preference. And SID of the target item is forcibly fully masked (the last 𝑚 tokens are replaced with [M] ), ensuring the alignment with the inference task. Additionally, in the time-aware token masking stage, a normalized temporal embedding is further incorporated into the model’s input, providing explicit temporal information and strengthening the model’s sensitivity to interaction order. ## 4.4 Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis 

To investigate the sensitivity of TDPM to key hyperparameters and provide practical guidance for the model tuning, we conduct systematic hyperparameter analysis on Beauty dataset. Specifically, we examine the impact of the following hyperparameters: the base masking probability 𝛼 preventing the masking probability 𝑝 𝑗 from vanishing, the scaling factor 𝛽 controlling the overall contribution of the fused user preference, the initial value 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 of the adap-tive weight 𝜆 balancing the relative contributions of the period preference and the point preference, and the growth curvature 𝑘 

governing how 𝜆 evolves with the progression of training. 

4.4.1 Masking Probability. The masking probability 𝑝 𝑗 in the time-aware token masking stage is jointly governed by two hyperparam-eters: the base masking probability 𝛼 and the scaling factor 𝛽 , as defined in Equation 10. Specifically, 𝛼 provides the lower bound that prevents 𝑝 𝑗 from degenerating to zero in extreme cases, thereby en-suring a minimum level of perturbation across all interacted items. In contrast, 𝛽 controls the overall contribution of period preference and point preference, effectively determining the dynamic range over which 𝑝 𝑗 can vary in response to the preference signals. To bet-ter comprehend how these two hyperparameters jointly shape the masking probability and consequently the recommendation pref-erence, we conduct a grid search over 𝛼 and 𝛽 on Beauty dataset, reporting the recommendation performance of each configuration. As shown in Figure 6, with both 𝛼 and 𝛽 set as {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}, we notice that the best performance tends to be achieved when 

𝛽 is larger than 𝛼 (i.e., the lower triangle of the heatmap). This phenomenon implies that the fused preference modeling should be given a relatively higher weight and if 𝛽 is much less than 𝛼 (e.g., 

𝛼 = 0.5 & 𝛽 = 0.1), the model can hardly capture the user preference. Notably, when both 𝛼 and 𝛽 set to large values (e.g., 𝛼 = 0.5 &

𝛽 = 0.5), the final masking probability 𝑝 𝑗 becomes excessively high, leading to a significant performance degradation. This decline can be attributed to the severe perturbation of historical interaction information: with too few visible tokens remaining in the masked Conference acronym ’XX, June 03–05, 2018, Woodstock, NY Bangguo Zhu, Peng Huo, Yuanbo Zhao, Zhicheng Du, Jun Yin, and Senzhang Wang 

SID sequence, the model receives extremely insufficient historical context to accurately capture the underlying user preferences. 

4.4.2 Weighted Preference Fusion. The fusion of period preference and point preference at the time-aware token masking stage is con-trolled by two critical hyperparameters: the initial value 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 of the adaptive weight 𝜆 and the growth curvature 𝑘 , as specified in Equation 11. Concretely, 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 determines the starting contribution ratio of period preference 𝑝 𝑝𝑒𝑟𝑖𝑜𝑑 relative to the point preference 

𝑝 𝑝𝑜𝑖𝑛𝑡 at the beginning of the time-aware token masking stage, while 𝑘 governs how rapidly this ratio evolves with the progres-sion of training. To comprehend the sensitivity of TDPM to these two hyperparameters and identify the optimal fusion schedule, we vary 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 and 𝑘 individually on Beauty dataset, reporting the recommendation performance of each configuration. As shown in Figure 7, with 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 and 𝑘 set as {0.3, 0.5, 0.8} and {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} respectively, we notice that the best performance tends to be achieved when 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 is set as a medium value and 𝑘 is set as a large value (e.g., 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 = 0.5 & 𝑘 = 2.0). The results reveal two crucial insights. First, a moderately balanced initialization of 𝜆 

(e.g., 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 ≈ 0.5) allows the model to effectively learn both period preference and point preference, but this benefit materializes only when paired with a large 𝑘 . When 𝑘 is set to a small value (e.g., 

𝑘 = 0.5), the fusion weight shifts too rapidly at the early stage, caus-ing that 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 = 0.5 cannot balance the relative contributions of period preference and point preference sufficiently, leading to infe-rior performance. Second, a large 𝑘 (e.g., 𝑘 = 2.0) yields consistently superior results by maintaining the balanced fusion throughout most of the training epochs and concentrating on the transition toward period preference in the final epochs. The optimal config-uration is therefore 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 = 0.5 combined with 𝑘 = 2.0, which achieves the best overall performance on Beauty dataset. 

## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we bridge the gap between the uniform forward diffu-sion process of standard diffusion model and the non-uniform user preference distribution in sequential recommendation by Prefer-ence Disentanglement and Time-Aware Diffusion . Specifically, we first disentangle user preference from a temporal perspective into period preference and point preference, explicitly modeling both enduring preference consistency and abrupt interest devia-tions. Building upon this disentangled preference modeling, we propose the time-aware token diffusion, realized as time-aware token masking that dynamically modulates the masking probabil-ity of each interacted item according to the disentangled period preference and point preference, fundamentally departing from the indiscriminate uniform diffusion adopted by prior SID-based diffusion models. Extensive experiments on three public real-world datasets demonstrate the necessity and superiority of the proposed time-aware token diffusion for SID-based diffusion models. 

## A Implementation Detail 

SID Generation. This study employs the open source large lan-guage model Qwen3-Embedding-8B [37 ] pretrained for embedding generation and developed by Alibaba to encode each item’s textural metadata into 4096-dimensional embedding vector, which is kept frozen in subsequent clustering. We then apply Residual Quantiza-tion K-Means algorithm over the entire set of the embedding vectors with 𝑚 = 4 quantization layers, each containing | C (𝑘 ) |= 256 clus-tering centroids for 𝑘 = 1, . . . , 𝑚 , resulting a 4-token SID for each item. All SIDs are collected into the vocabulary and a prefix tree (Trie) is constructed to enable the constrained beam search. 

Model Architecture. Following [ 24 ], we employ an 8-layer encoder-only transformer model with a hidden dimension of 128, rotary position embedding (RoPE) and 8 attention heads. A multi-layer perceptron with a hidden size of 3072 is used. The total number of trainable parameters is approximately 7M .

Training Configuration. We train our TDPM using the AdamW optimizer [ 18 ] with a learning rate of 3 × 10 −3, weight decay of 

1 × 10 −3 and a batch size of 128. Usually, the warm-up stage lasts for 30-60 epochs, during which masking probability 𝑝 is sampled from the bounded uniform distribution U ( 0.0, 0.5). For period pref-erence modeling, we employ a simple temporal index distribution function 𝑡 ( 𝑗 ) = 𝑗 , adopt z-score normalization function as Norm (·) 

to center and scale the temporal indices, and standard Sigmoid function 𝜎 = 11+𝑒 −𝑥 as the squashing function 𝜙 𝑡 . And for period preference modeling, we employ cosine similarity as 𝑠𝑖𝑚 (· , ·) to measure the semantic proximity between adjacent items followed by a Max-Min normalization function to scale the output into [0, 1] as 𝜙 𝑑 . The time-aware token masking stage runs for an additional 60-90 epochs with 𝛼 = 0.1, 𝛽 = 0.3, 𝜆 𝑠𝑡𝑎𝑟𝑡 = 0.5 and 𝑘 = 2.0.
